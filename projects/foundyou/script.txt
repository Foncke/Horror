If you're ever hiking alone in the woods, and realize the forest has gone completely silent... do not look into the trees. Just turn around, and walk out.

Last October, I took a solo trip deep into the mountains. I missed a turn on the ridge, and before I knew it, the sun was gone. I wasn't panicking yet. I had a flashlight, a compass, and plenty of water. I just needed to find the main trail.

That's when I realized... the woods had gone dead silent. No wind. No birds. Not even the crunch of leaves under my boots sounded right.

I swept my flashlight across the treeline, looking for a marker... and froze.

Twenty feet ahead, half-hidden behind a massive pine, someone was standing completely still... staring right at me.

I yelled out, asking if they were lost. They didn't answer. They didn't even blink when my beam hit their face. Instead, their head slowly tilted sideways — way past what a human neck could do — until their ear rested flat against their shoulder.

Then, from deep in the woods behind me, I heard my own voice yell back... "Are you lost?"

I spun around, flashing my light into the empty dark behind me. My heart was hammering against my ribs. When I whipped the beam back toward the pine tree... the figure was gone.

I stood there shaking, sweeping my light in every direction. Until I felt warm breath on the back of my neck... and my own voice whispered right into my ear... "Found you."
