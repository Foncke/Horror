In Thailand, the scariest monster doesn't hide in the woods... or wait in the dark under your bed.

It wears the skin of your neighbor... your partner... or the person sleeping in the room right next to yours.

It's called a Phi Pop. A parasitic, flesh-eating spirit from Isan, in northeastern Thailand.

Unlike ordinary ghosts, a Phi Pop isn't the spirit of someone who died. It is a living curse — born when forbidden dark magic backfires, rotting a human soul until something inhuman crawls inside to take its place.

By day, the host looks and acts completely normal. They smile, they hold conversations, and they eat dinner right beside you.

Until the house goes quiet after midnight.

That's when the parasite wakes up. It hijacks the host's nervous system — forcing them to creep into the dark to hunt for raw meat, warm blood, and fresh human entrails. And the worst part? Folklore warns the victim is sometimes trapped inside their own mind... watching helplessly as their hands tear into raw flesh.

If it can't find prey outside, it slowly eats its own host from the inside out, hollowing out their organs until they waste away... before jumping into someone new through a single drop of saliva.

Elders say there are only two ways to tell if someone you love is already gone.

They will never look you directly in the eye... and they always wake up starving at three A.M.

So tonight, if you hear chewing in the kitchen... don't turn on the light.
