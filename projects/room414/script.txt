Imagine you check into a hotel at 3 a.m.

The man at the front desk never looks up. He just slides you a key. Room 414.

The hallway is so long, the lights at the end look like stars. Every door is shut.

On your pillow, there's a card. No logo. No phone number. Just one rule:

"If someone knocks after midnight, do not answer. We do not have night staff."

You laugh.

Then — knock. Knock. Knock.

"Room service."

You didn't order anything.

You look through the peephole. A bellhop. Perfectly still. Smiling… too wide.

He doesn't knock again. He just moves to the next door. Knock. Knock. Knock.

And it opens.

Then the next one. And the next. Every guest steps out… and follows him down the hall.

Until the hallway is empty.

Then, behind you — from inside your bathroom — someone whispers:

"Don't let him know you're awake."

You checked in alone.

Under your door, the light goes dark. Two shadows. He's back.

So you have two choices.

Open the door… and follow the others.

Or stay… with whatever is in your bathroom.

What would you do?
