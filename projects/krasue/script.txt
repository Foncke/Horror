In Thailand, there is an ancient curse so horrifying that villagers still hang thorny branches outside their windows at night.

She looks like an ordinary, quiet woman living down the street. She smiles at neighbors, buys groceries, and blends into the crowd.

They call her a Krasue.

By day, she is completely human. But the moment midnight falls... her body splits apart.

Her head detaches from her neck, rising silently into the night sky — trailing her glowing, pulsing heart, stomach, and dripping intestines beneath her.

Driven by an insatiable hunger, the Krasue floats through the dark in search of raw meat, stagnant blood, and newborn babies.

If you look out your window at three A.M., you might mistake her for a floating lantern drifting through the trees... until she turns around, and you see a severed head staring back with hollow, starving eyes.

Before sunrise, she must return to her headless body, reattaching herself before anyone notices. Because if her body is hidden or destroyed while she hunts, she dies in agony at dawn.

Elders say there is only one way to spot a Krasue in daylight.

She will look exhausted, avoid direct sunlight, and never touch cooked food.

So tonight, if you see a faint red glow hovering outside your bedroom window... don't open the curtains.
